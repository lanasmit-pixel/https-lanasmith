The Michigan tax surprise

Budget for the tax bill after the sale.

A property's tax bill is based on taxable value multiplied by the local millage rate. Under Proposal A, taxable value can generally rise only by the lesser of 5% or inflation each year. When a home sells, the cap is lifted and the taxable value resets based on the home's SEV in the year after the sale.

The previous owner's lower tax bill does not simply transfer to the buyer. Plan for the post-purchase tax estimate when building your budget.

After you get the keys

Two things to do after closing

1. File the Property Transfer Affidavit
Submit Form L-4260 to the local assessor within 45 days of the transfer.

2. Apply for your Principal Residence Exemption
If this is your primary residence, ask about Form 2368 and the current filing deadline.

Confirm current filing rules with your local assessor.

Help with up-front costs

Explore Michigan down-payment assistance.

Ask a participating lender about MSHDA MI Home Loan and MI $10K DPA. Programs can help with down payment and closing costs, subject to current eligibility rules.

The assistance is typically a 0% second mortgage with no monthly payment, repaid when the home is sold, refinanced, or paid off.

Requirements can include income limits, credit requirements, purchase-price limits, a minimum contribution, homebuyer education, and liquid-asset limits. Ann Arbor homes can run above MSHDA price limits, so ask early whether the homes you are considering qualify.

Also ask about MI Home Loan Flex, Mortgage Credit Certificates, and local, bank, or employer programs. Program rules, limits, and deadlines change, so confirm current terms.

The full cash picture

Budget for more than the down payment.

  • Closing costs, typically around 2–5% of the purchase price
  • Earnest money deposit
  • Inspection and appraisal fees
  • Prepaid taxes, insurance, and escrow reserves
  • Moving costs and immediate repairs

Michigan transfer taxes are customarily paid by the seller, but who pays what is negotiable and should be stated in the contract.
